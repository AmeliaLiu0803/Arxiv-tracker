GBClaw: An agentic AI framework for atomistic grain boundary research and mechanistic discovery
Authors: Yazhuo Liu, Kunqing Ding, Xiaoguo Gong, Zifeng Wang, Yichen Yang, Yifeng Che, Wen Chen, Josh Kacher, Olivier Pierron, Shigenobu Ogata, Ting Zhu
First: 2026-10-20T17:59:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:59:40+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Agentic artificial intelligence (AI) systems hold immense potential for automating complex workflows in materials discovery and design, but extending them to domain-intensive mechanistic investigations remains challenging. Here, we present GBClaw, an agentic AI framework for investigating the structure and deformation of general grain boundaries (GBs). Although general GBs constitute the vast majority of real-world interfaces in polycrystalline materials, they remain understudied because their structural complexity and interfacial mismatch complicate experimental and modeling research. GBClaw integrates large language models (LLMs) into a modular architecture comprising an orchestrator, specialized agents, domain-specific tools, simulation interfaces, and hierarchical memory. Through dynamic replanning and iterative workflow refinement, the framework effectively connects systematic simulations with targeted mechanistic analysis. Applied to 14 general asymmetric tilt GBs in nickel, GBClaw characterized interfacial defect structures and identified diverse deformation modes across these incommensurate boundaries. Comparing boundary sliding with and without secondary GB misfit dislocations revealed substantial differences in shear strength, underscoring the critical role of defect hierarchies in sliding resistance. Furthermore, researcher-guided follow-up calculations traced an anomalously high shear resistance to metastable boundary structures. This work demonstrates that combining agentic AI with domain expertise bridges high-throughput computational screening with deep physical understanding of complex material interfaces.

Semantic Observability for Autonomous Systems Diagnostics
Authors: Jitendra Solanki, Anand Todkar, Ziran Min
First: 2026-10-20T17:53:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:53:32+00:00
Comments: 8 pages, 3 figures. Foundational architecture paper; companion evaluation paper forthcoming
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Modern distributed microservice deployments generate massive volumes of heterogeneous, multi-format observability logs across physically and logically partitioned infrastructure. Isolating root causes during cascading failures remains an arduous task for Site Reliability Engineers (SREs), bottlenecked by manual keyword filtering and disjointed temporal correlation across monitoring dashboards. We introduce Semantic Observability, an autonomous multi-agent framework that unifies disparate log streams into a dynamic, queryable Property Graph Database. By modeling physical infrastructure topologies, logical software dependencies, and chronological event sequences as attributed nodes and directed relations, it bridges the semantic gap inherent in flat, unstructured log repositories. Building on this unified substrate, we deploy a stateful multi-agent system orchestrated through Directed Cyclic Graphs (DCGs). The system autonomously navigates the topology, applying causal graph algorithms and temporal back-traversal to isolate the precise blast radius of anomalies. The pruned subgraph is then interpreted by a Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning engine to generate contextualized, code-level Root Cause Analyses (RCA) and actionable remediations. This paper establishes the foundational system architecture, formal relational ontology, and multi-agent control loops of Semantic Observability. We show conceptually how the framework eliminates ambient telemetry noise, reduces Mean Time to Resolution (MTTR), compresses LLM token consumption, and prevents structural hallucinations. We also specify an open-source validation framework on standard benchmarks (LogHub and Train-Ticket), laying the groundwork for a forthcoming companion paper on exhaustive quantitative evaluation and production benchmarking.

Benchmarking Arabic Dialects for Math Problems
Authors: Noor Abo Mokh, Teresa Lynn, Nizar Habash, Ahmed Elshabrawy, Sara Alhajeri, Alaa Elsetohy, Preslav Nakov, Alham Fikri Aji
First: 2026-10-20T17:50:13+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:50:13+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Mathematical reasoning remains underexplored for low-resource languages, including Dialectal Arabic. To bridge this gap, we introduce the DAR-MGSM (Dialectal Arabic Multilingual Grade School Math) Benchmark, the first manually curated benchmark for evaluating mathematical reasoning in Dialectal Arabic. DAR-MGSM consists of math word problems (MWPs) requiring multi-step reasoning and covers five major Arabic dialects alongside Modern Standard Arabic (MSA). The benchmark comprises two parallel versions: translated-only and translated & culturally localized. The latter version adapts entities and references to regional varieties. We evaluate eleven Arabic-centric and multilingual large language models on DAR-MGSM. The results show that the performance generally declines on the localized version, highlighting the challenges introduced by culturally grounded adaptations. However, the magnitude of this effect varies across models, with Arabic-centric models exhibiting different levels of sensitivity to localization. A mechanistic interpretability analysis shows that translated and localized variants of the same MWPs often induce distinct internal reasoning trajectories, even when aggregate accuracy remains similar. Overall, our results show that mathematical reasoning performance depends on both linguistic variety and cultural context.

Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) tend to generate a long reasoning chain when solving complex tasks. However, as the reasoning chain extends, critical intermediate steps and the original prompt will be buried in the context, receiving insufficient attention and leading to errors. In this work, we present ATAR, a novel reasoning method that leverages the inherent reasoning structure to steer LLM attention. Our experiments show that ATAR outperforms SOTA methods across six benchmarks, achieving up to 15.39% absolute improvement. Furthermore, with ATAR, "non-reasoning" models achieve comparable or even better performance compared to reasoning models of the same size in most benchmarks. Finally, our ablation studies show that the attention alignment component contributes significantly, and that these improvements are persist under different attentionsteering backends.

The Token-Energy Law of LLM Decode: Active Bytes and Realized Time Account for Joules per Token
Authors: Chenxu Niu, Batuhan Sencer, Wei Zhang, Yong Chen
First: 2026-10-20T17:32:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:32:08+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Energy-efficient deployment of large language models requires understanding how model architecture, hardware, and serving settings shape energy per generated token. We introduce the Token-Energy Law, a compact, interpretable model of GPU-board energy per output token for dense, full-history decoding. It relates token energy to two core dimensions: active bytes and realized decode time. Active bytes describe logical byte demand per output, separating dense weights shared within a decode iteration from private key--value (KV) histories read and updated for each request. Realized decode time is the elapsed decode duration divided by the number of output tokens produced. We combine the two byte coordinates and time in a common affine form, with coefficients calibrated for each model and hardware/runtime configuration. This formulation explains how batching amortizes shared-weight work, longer attended histories increase private KV demand, and throughput determines the time-associated cost per output. Our experiments cover three models under BF16 decoding on NVIDIA Hopper and Blackwell GPUs, including two-GPU tensor parallelism on Hopper. Using fixed coefficients and observed decode time, held-out condition-mean energy estimates differ from measurements by at most 3.73%.

When Do LLMs Help with Feature Selection? A Systematic Empirical Study
Authors: Fateme Memar, Dongjie Wang, Arvin Agah

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) can leverage semantic feature names alongside summary statistics for tabular feature selection, yet it remains unclear when such information improves subset selection relative to established statistical methods. We investigate this question within an 18-dataset framework comprising 10 datasets for Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) training, 2 for checkpoint validation, and 6 held-out evaluation datasets. We compare five classical baselines, frozen Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.3-70B selectors, and two GRPO-adapted Qwen2.5-7B models, reporting classification ROC-AUC and regression R^2 separately and using dataset-wise ranks for cross-task comparison. Across five seeded evaluation partitions, classical selectors generally achieve higher predictive performance than the tested 7B LLM/RL selectors, including under a pre-specified ~33% feature budget with strict train-only classical fitting, while the 7B selectors retain compact subsets. A cardinality-matched semantic sensitivity experiment shows that, for Zero-shot Qwen-7B, informative feature names improve regression performance over anonymized and shuffled alternatives (+0.1964 and +0.1606 mean R^2), whereas aggregate classification differences are smaller (+0.0159 and +0.0084 ROC-AUC) and dataset-dependent. These results characterize when semantic information benefits LLM-based feature selection and show that classical selectors remain strong baselines for predictive tabular feature selection.

Decomposing Refusal Steering in Mixture-of-Experts Models
Authors: Anna C. Marbut, Daniel R. Olson, Travis J. Wheeler
First: 2026-06-02T19:23:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T16:57:45+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Safety alignment in instruction-tuned large language models (LLMs) depends on a model's ability to reliably refuse harmful or disallowed requests. Recent work has shown that a steering vector can be applied to a dense LLM during inference to suppress refusal behavior and induce responses to harmful requests. We extend this refusal steering method to three open-source Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs to decompose and better understand how refusal mechanisms operate across MoE components. We find that steering performance is uninhibited by the complex routing patterns inherent to the MoE architecture, and that a single expert recovers 78% of the full steering effect on average when free to select its own location. However, expert-level steering only recovers 54% on average when constrained to the location used by full-layer steering, a gap that widens further under safety-related system prompts. Our results also show that refusal signals captured by these steering methods differ from those captured by expert routing behavior. Together, these results suggest that refusal behavior is distributed unevenly across the MoE LLM architecture rather than governed by a single unified mechanism.

BitMTP: When Multi-Token Prediction Meets Low-Bit Large Language Models
Authors: Ning Zhang, Shihao Wang, Jinrui Zhang, Chaodong Xiao, Lei Zhang
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-20T16:42:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T16:42:17+00:00
Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. 16 pages, 3 figures. Code: https://github.com/PolyU-VCLab/BitMTP
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
For efficient deployment of large language models (LLMs), methods of ultra-low-bit backbones reduce weight storage, memory traffic, and linear-layer execution cost, while multi-token prediction (MTP) methods reduce sequential decoding steps by predicting multiple future tokens in parallel. Although complementary, the direct combination of these methods cannot accelerate decoding at comparable quality: masked drafting lacks intermediate token input supplied in next-token training, while wider computation and causal key-value (KV) updates can offset the savings from fewer steps. To address these challenges, we propose BitMTP, an algorithm-system co-design framework for efficient MTP on ultra-low-bit LLM backbones. A joint next-token prediction and MTP objective equips a single model with both autoregressive (AR) generation and block-causal drafting. Confidence-only decoding, speculative-only decoding, and hybrid decoding provide different quality-throughput operating points without using separate draft and verifier models. Accepted-prefix alignment keeps the committed prefix, materialized causal KV boundary, and CUDA Graph replay state consistent for continued generation. BitMTP uses this contract to select width-specialized low-bit execution and reuse captured graphs over shared KV storage. On NVIDIA H20, BitMTP with hybrid decoding achieves five-task mean speedups of 2.2x on BitNet b1.58-2B and 2.1-2.4x on 1.7B-4B Bonsai models at comparable task quality. On RTX 3090 and Jetson Orin, it further achieves up to 2.0x in five-task mean throughput and 3.3x on MBPP+, respectively. These results demonstrate that BitMTP accelerates ultra-low-bit LLM generation across backbones, model sizes, and GPU types. The source code is released at https://github.com/PolyU-VCLab/BitMTP

Claims of emergence in AI should be grounded in information decomposition
Authors: Christoph Riedl, Fernando E. Rosas
Venue: NeurIPS Spotlight
First: 2026-10-20T16:29:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T16:29:28+00:00
Comments: NeurIPS Spotlight Paper
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
When a language model develops capabilities not traceable to its individual components, when a multi-agent AI system outperforms each agent on its own, or when a human-AI pair succeeds where either fails in isolation, AI researchers describe it as a case of ``synergy'' or ``emergence''. However, these are usually informal assessments not supported by a theoretical framework, making these claims impossible to compare or even meaningfully dispute. Here we argue that \emph{information decomposition can serve as a unifying formal framework to operationalize assessments about synergy in AI systems}. Information decomposition provides a way to distinguish qualitatively different \emph{types} of information, including unique information available from individual components, redundant information shared across multiple components, and synergistic information accessible only from parts taken together. This gives claims of emergence a precise, falsifiable criterion: a system's behavior is emergent only if it carries synergistic information about task-relevant outcomes. We apply this lens to attention heads, autonomous agents, and human-AI teams, showing how it organizes existing knowledge across scales and substrates, while also generating cross-level conjectures that siloed fields would miss.

Knowledge Acquisition During Pre-training? Large Language Models Learn Better With Auxiliary Views
Authors: Joseph Lee, Yidi Huang, Dokyoon Kim, Shu Yang, Li Shen
Venue: EMNLP 2026
First: 2026-09-03T17:57:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T16:21:50+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Gaps remain in our understanding of how large language models (LLMs) acquire knowledge during pre-training. We find that auxiliary views, conceptual reformulations, are key to this process. We design controlled experiments to isolate this. First, holding the token budget fixed, allocating tokens from paraphrases to auxiliary views improves learning even for factual recall, which we use near-verbatim sentences from the original document to test. Second, the effectiveness of auxiliary views is not contingent on the strength of the model that generates them. Third, we establish that paraphrasing is helpful but only at smaller batch sizes. Fourth, we identify types of knowledge that aid learning in the presence of prior knowledge gaps. Finally, we examine how these effects manifest mechanistically via layer-wise biases and compression. Together, our findings suggest that auxiliary representations of knowledge are a key factor in the success of pre-training, and explains why data diversity matters. Further, our results provide clear recipes for synthetic data augmentation along with implications on the self-improvement in LLMs.

Beyond Trajectory Statistics: Community-Consistent Human Mobility Generation via Evolving User-Location Graphs
Authors: Bo Liu, Tong Li, Jiapeng Zhang, Zhu Xiao, Zhuo Tang, Kenli Li

Abstract
Human mobility data are fundamental to Web-based location services and broader urban applications, and mobility generation provides synthetic trajectories when real data are costly or inaccessible. Although recent mobility generators have incorporated collective mobility patterns, they primarily focus on individual trajectories and aggregate population dynamics, leaving evolving community structures formed by user-location interactions insufficiently modeled. In this paper, we study community-consistent human mobility generation. To this end, we formulate mobility generation as the generation of dynamic user-location graphs to model community structures and propose MOTION, a Multi-agent framework for cOmmuniTy-consIstent human mObility geNeration. MOTION consists of user agents, location agents, and a global manager agent. MOTION first detects coherent communities from historical mobility using the Leiden algorithm and extracts community-conditioned interaction priors to guide the generation process. Guided by these priors, the user agents plan mobility, search destination candidates, and infer user movements from the individual view. In parallel, selectively activated location agents infer likely user arrivals from the community view. The manager coordinates both views, diagnoses interactions causing deviations, and attributes targeted reflection signals to revise user decisions, location attraction, and source-user selection. Experiments on two real-world datasets show that MOTION outperforms baselines by at least 4.05% across reported metrics of individual trajectories, population and flow distributions, and community and graph structures. Further analyses confirm its robustness, privacy preservation, and downstream utility. The code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/MOTION-04C4.

The Trilemma of Truth in Large Language Models
Authors: Germans Savcisens, Tina Eliassi-Rad
First: 2025-06-30T14:49:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T16:04:02+00:00

Abstract
The public often attributes human-like qualities to large language models (LLMs), assuming that they "know" certain things. In reality, LLMs encode information retained during training as internal probabilistic knowledge. This study examines existing methods for probing the veracity of that knowledge and identifies three flawed underlying assumptions. To address these flaws, we introduce sAwMIL (Sparse-Aware Multiple-Instance Learning), a multiclass probing framework that combines multiple-instance learning with conformal prediction. sAwMIL leverages LLMs' internal representations to classify statements as true, false, or neither. We evaluate sAwMIL across 16 open-source LLMs, including default and chat-based variants, using three new curated datasets. Our results show that (1) common probing methods fail to provide a reliable and transferable veracity direction and, in some settings, perform worse than zero-shot prompting; (2) truth and falsehood are not encoded symmetrically; and (3) LLMs encode a third type of signal that is distinct from both true and false.

Stratified Sparse Attention: Reducing the Sparse-to-Dense Gap at Extreme Sparsity
Authors: Sahil Joshi, Prithvi Dixit, Aryan Katiyar, Agniva Chowdhury, Joseph E. Gonzalez, Anshumali Shrivastava, Aditya Desai, Kumar Krishna Agrawal
First: 2026-10-20T16:02:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T16:02:12+00:00
Comments: 26 pages, 5 figures, 13 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Large Language Model inference over long contexts is increasingly bottlenecked by the cost of attending to a growing Key-Value (KV) cache. Sparse attention reduces this cost by accessing only a fraction of the cache, but existing methods face a trade-off between deterministic retrieval, which captures high-scoring tokens but discards residual attention mass, and sampling, which improves coverage at the cost of higher variance. Hybrid methods combine top-$k$ retrieval with uniform sampling, yet still exhibit a substantial gap from dense attention at aggressive sparsity. We introduce \emph{Stratified Sparse Attention (SSA)}, a unified framework combining deterministic retrieval with score-guided sampling. SSA deterministically attends to high-scoring tokens while sampling the residual using proxy attention scores and correcting for their inclusion probabilities, thereby recovering attention mass beyond the retrieval set. SSA encompasses top-$k$, top-$p$, and hybrid top-$k$+sampling as special cases. Across RULER, LongBench, LOFT, and AIME, and five models ranging from 3B to 27B parameters, SSA consistently improves over the baselines at matched sparsity. On RULER-HARD with Ministral-3-8B, SSA improves accuracy over the strongest practical baseline from 81.8\% to 84.2\% at 32K and from 63.8\% to 69.3\% at 128K under $50\times$ attention sparsity, recovering 94\% and 85\% of dense-attention performance, respectively. With optimized CUDA kernels, SSA achieves up to $2.96\times$ decode speedup over FlashInfer at 1M context, demonstrating that its accuracy gains translate into practical long-context inference acceleration. We open-source our code at https://github.com/skylight-org/SSA-Paper-Code.

Every Model Leaves a Trace: Multi-Resolution LLM Fingerprinting
Authors: Mohammad Reza Modarres, Armin Tourajmehr, Artin Tavasoli, Yadollah Yaghoobzadeh, Mohammad Taher Pilehvar
First: 2026-10-20T16:00:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T16:00:34+00:00

Abstract
Large language models leave model-specific traces in their generated text, but it remains unclear how finely these fingerprints can distinguish related models and which signals remain informative as obvious cues are reduced. We study this through fingerprint resolution, considering both model resolution, from different families to distilled and adapted variants, and signal resolution across behavioral, stylometric, lexical, and semantic evidence. We progressively suppress surface cues, paraphrase outputs, vary the amount of available text, and test generalization across prompt distributions and languages. Our results show that fingerprints persist beyond broad family differences, remaining detectable in distilled models and even LoRA adaptations of the same base model. Although attribution weakens as surface and lexical cues are altered, substantial model-specific signal remains. These signals transfer across prompt sources and languages, with semantic representations proving most robust across languages. These findings show that model identity is recoverable at multiple resolutions, but that recoverability depends on model similarity and the evidence available in the text.

Abstract
The increasing complexity of Industrial Control Systems (ICS) and their integration with IT infrastructures has significantly expanded the cyber threat landscape. Traditional cybersecurity models, primarily based on the Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (CIA triad), fail to capture the broader societal and operational impacts of cyberattacks. Abstract
Code-based video generation offers fine-grained control, targeted editing, and component reuse compared with direct video generation. This approach requires multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to translate visual and temporal requirements into executable programs that produce complete videos. However, existing code-based benchmarks primarily target specialized tasks, leaving complete video creation across diverse creative categories and input modalities insufficiently evaluated. We introduce Code2Video-Compass, a general-purpose benchmark for code-based video generation across text-to-video (T2V), image-to-video (I2V), and video-to-video (V2V) settings. Code2Video-Compass comprises 2,104 tasks spanning 10 categories, 24 subtypes, and three complexity dimensions covering construction, dynamic animation, and state dependency. It combines tasks curated from textual instructions and visual references with corresponding T2V, I2V, and V2V tasks derived from complete professional and user-created videos. We propose a dual-level evaluation framework combining 12 objective metrics and five subjective metrics. Objective evaluation covers operational performance, video quality, text-video alignment, and reference fidelity, while subjective evaluation uses an MLLM judge to evaluate instruction following and holistic video quality. Extensive evaluation of 15 recent proprietary and open-weight MLLMs from nine families reveals that even leading models face challenges in reliably generating high-quality complete videos aligned with textual and visual requirements, with performance generally declining as construction, dynamic animation, and state-dependency complexity increases. We anticipate that Code2Video-Compass will advance reliable, coherent, and controllable code-based video generation and inspire broader progress in programmatic visual creation.

Rewards for Budget-Aware Multi-Turn Agents That Adapt to Arbitrary Test-Time Constraints
Authors: Tom A. Lamb, Adam Golinski, Eleonora Gualdoni, Sinead Williamson, Michael Kirchhof
First: 2026-10-20T15:49:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T15:49:32+00:00

Abstract
Large language model agents are often given an inference budget and asked to maximise success within it. A multi-turn agent has to allocate this budget across many environment interactions. An external harness can enforce the budget, but it remains unclear whether the agent itself can learn when and how much to spend. We ask whether agents can learn this allocation across dynamically specified budgets and which rewards best teach this behaviour. We adapt existing length penalties to budget constraints spanning multiple resource types, introduce new reward formulations, and compare eleven objectives in a shared training pipeline. We train Qwen3.5 models at 4B and 9B on AppWorld with budgets over tokens, API calls, and environment steps, and on the BabyAI gridworld with turn budgets. We train a separate policy for each environment and resource type, with each policy trained across budget values. Training with rewards that penalise overspending substantially improves within-budget success and natural compliance over untrained models and over training on task success alone, with agents learning to adapt their resource use to stated budgets and task difficulty. These gains extend to unseen budgets and, zero-shot, to a shifted task distribution and a new benchmark. Reward objectives trade off success within budget and natural compliance. A sigmoid penalty for overspending most often achieves the highest within-budget success at an individual budget, while a fixed negative reward for overspending generally yields the strongest natural compliance. Combining penalties for overspend frequency and magnitude gives the smallest average gap to the best method at each budget. Overall, we show that a single agent can learn to allocate resources across turns and adapt its behaviour to user-specified budgets using budget feedback without external budget-allocation controllers.

DIVE: Benchmarking Full-Duplex Interaction with Visual Engagement in Continuous Streams
Authors: Yuhang Chen, Guangyue Liu, Shilong Liu
First: 2026-10-20T15:48:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T15:48:29+00:00
Comments: Preprint. 19 pages, 4 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recent advances in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have substantially improved spoken interaction and video understanding. Existing online and streaming benchmarks primarily assess causal multimodal perception through timestamped question answering, whereas real-world interaction requires models to continuously identify task-relevant multimodal cues and decide whether, when, and how to respond. This leaves open how well current models can sustain real-time interaction throughout an evolving multimodal stream. To address this gap, we introduce DIVE, a benchmark for evaluating full-duplex interaction with visual engagement under continuous and realistic multimodal streams. It organizes six tasks into three complementary interaction scenarios. (1) Proactive Intervention evaluates whether models can identify emerging visual or semantic cues and intervene at the appropriate moment. (2) Continuous Engagement evaluates sustained participation through continuous visual tracking and visually grounded query interaction. (3) Interaction Coordination evaluates how models coordinate listening and speaking under overlapping and multi-party interactions. DIVE contains 525 audio/video samples with fine-grained human annotations and precise temporal metadata. Its evaluation protocol separates response quality, participation decisions, and response timing. We evaluate representative real-time models using response quality, window response rates, and reaction latency. Experiments show that leading real-time models achieve sub-second average response latency, yet their average window response rates remain at or below 53.77%. Response quality varies substantially across models and tasks, with adapting ongoing responses to newly arriving information remaining particularly challenging. Abstract

Abstract
Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly use persistent memory to personalize responses, and personal attributes in this memory pose a privacy risk if exposed. We ask how consulting memory during dialogue changes what users go on to say, and thus what the memory comes to reveal. In multi-session simulations, a simulated user with 24 personal attributes talks with a support agent that does or does not consult memory, and in both settings attributes are inferred from the full accumulated record.
