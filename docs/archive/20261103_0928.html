Latent Reasoning in Continuous Space for Unified Multimodal Models
Authors: Byungwoo Jeon, Yoonwoo Jeong, Hyunseok Lee, Minsu Cho, Jinwoo Shin
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-30T17:59:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:59:43+00:00
Comments: Published as conference proceeding for NeurIPS 2026. Last two authors advised equally
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) achieve remarkable success on diverse multimodal understanding and generation tasks, yet they still struggle to effectively leverage language-based chain-of-thought reasoning for image generation. As a result, existing reasoning-augmented UMMs often fail to inherit the test-time scaling and fine-grained controllability observed in large language models. We attribute this failure to a fundamental modality mismatch: reasoning is performed in a discrete token space, whereas image generation operates in a continuous space. To bridge this gap, we introduce a Latent Reasoning framework in Continuous space (LARC), which alleviates the token-level bottleneck by reasoning over continuous hidden states. Specifically, the training framework consists of two stages: (i) curriculum supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and (ii) information-gain reinforcement learning (RL). First, the curriculum SFT stage gradually converts token-level reasoning into latent reasoning. Subsequently, the information-gain RL stage self-evolves latent reasoning without ground truth supervision. Experiments across diverse text-to-image generation benchmarks show that LARC consistently improves generation quality, prompt following, and compositional fidelity. Notably, LARC achieves state-of-the-art performance on GenEval with a 7%p gain over BAGEL and demonstrates clear test-time scaling behavior, outperforming token-based generation under both parallel and sequential test-time scaling strategies.

Abstract
Domain models are central to software engineering, as they enable a shared understanding, guide implementation, and support automated analyses and model-driven development. Yet, despite these benefits, practitioners often skip modeling because it is time-consuming and demands scarce expertise. We address this barrier by investigating whether open-weight large language models, adapted via instruction tuning, can generate high-quality BPMN process models directly from natural language descriptions in a cost-effective and privacy-preserving way. We introduce InstruBPM, a reproducible approach that prepares paired text-diagram data and instruction tunes an open source large language model with parameter-efficient fine-tuning and quantization for on-prem deployment. We evaluate the tuned model through complementary perspectives: (i) text/code similarity using BLEU, ROUGE-L, and METEOR, (ii) structural fidelity using Relative Graph Edit Distance, (iii) guidelines conformance using external tool checks, and (iv) a small expert review. Using a curated subset of a multi-domain BPMN dataset, we compare the tuned model with untuned open-weight baselines and strong proprietary models under consistent prompting regimes. Our compact tuned model outperforms all baselines across sequence and structural metrics while requiring substantially fewer resources; guideline analysis and expert feedback further indicate that the generated diagrams largely follow BPMN best practices and are useful starting points that reduce modeling effort. Overall, instruction tuning improves structural accuracy and robustness compared to untuned baselines and reduces reliance on heavy prompt scaffolding. We publicly share the trained models and scripts to support reproducibility and further research.

ACCT: Asymmetric Cross-LLM Context Transfer
Authors: Yuzhe Liu, Haolin Tian, Tonghan Wang
First: 2026-10-30T17:50:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:50:49+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In real-world deployments, large language model (LLM) systems are increasingly evolving from single-model invocation toward heterogeneous deployments in which different models are dynamically invoked for inference. When the same context is reused across models, however, each newly selected target model typically has to process it again, repeatedly incurring substantial computational costs. Therefore, we propose ACCT (Asymmetric Cross-LLM Context Transfer), which targets KV cache transfer between models of different scales within the same model family. ACCT transforms the KV cache produced by a source model into sparse KV states that can be directly consumed by a target model, thereby enabling cross-model reuse of context computation and decoupling context computation from downstream inference. ACCT consists of three lightweight components: the Cartographer identifies query-relevant context tokens for each target layer and KV head; the Allocator determines how many context tokens each target layer and KV head retains under a shared total KV budget; and the Compiler transforms selected source KV into sparse target KV. The target model can therefore perform inference using the transferred KV without reprocessing the original context. We evaluate ACCT in two complementary settings comprising five general-capability benchmarks and six highly context-dependent tasks. Experiments show that ACCT matches or exceeds the target model's original inference performance even when reducing the KV cache size by 75%. At a context length of approximately 19K tokens, ACCT's KV conversion achieves a 6.5-21.2 times speedup over target context prefill. These results demonstrate the feasibility of transferring context computation across models under a limited KV budget.

Subquadratic Attention with a Hierarchical Tree-Structured Indexer
Authors: Leon Lufkin, Cutter Dawes, Alexander Speicher, Kamesh Krishnamurthy, Tomás Figliolia, Beren Millidge
First: 2026-10-30T17:48:37+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:48:37+00:00
Comments: 43 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The quadratic scaling of attention is a fundamental bottleneck limiting the long-context capabilities of large language models (LLMs). A variety of approaches have attempted to circumvent this, but they either fail to match attention's expressiveness (e.g., sliding-window or linear attention variants with fixed-size state) or only achieve constant-factor speedups (e.g., sparse attention methods whose indexers themselves scale quadratically). In this paper, we derive an asymptotically subquadratic sparse attention method based on a hierarchical, tree-structured indexer, which we call Zyphra Hierarchical Attention (ZHA). ZHA constructs a tree of recursively compressed key summaries over the KV cache, which future queries traverse from the top down to retrieve a sparse subset of keys. Crucially, by iteratively growing the tree's depth, the total cost including routing and final attention grows sublinearly in sequence length, resulting in overall subquadratic (but superlinear) scaling for attention. We demonstrate that ZHA can be cheaply applied to existing pretrained models with only a short KL distillation training stage over a few billion tokens, and maintains performance on long-context retrieval and challenging reasoning, mathematical, and agentic coding evaluation tasks. Moreover, we show that our method is highly robust to tree geometry including very deep trees of up to 6 levels. Finally, we develop a detailed performance model and show that our approach scales subquadratically, dramatically outperforms competing methods beyond 1 million context, and ultimately has the potential to extend effective context windows to the hundreds of millions or billions of tokens.

From Stages to States: Unified Iterative Reasoning for MLLMs
Authors: Chuang Yu, Xuefei Ma, Yuanhao Feng, Wuxiong Zeng, Bo Wang, Minghong Cai, Dongming Wu, Fei Zhou, Chen Tang, Wenwen Tong, Lewei Lu, Xiangyu Yue
First: 2026-10-11T13:46:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:40:17+00:00
Comments: Under review
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) substantially enhances the complex reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by explicitly modeling intermediate reasoning processes. However, most learning-based MCoT methods follow a stage-centric paradigm, where different reasoning behaviors are assigned to predefined stages and optimized separately. This results in fragmented reasoning and a training-inference reasoning-state gap. To address these limitations, we propose ReState, a unified iterative reasoning framework that reformulates multimodal reasoning from a stage-driven pipeline into a state-driven dynamic process. It comprises two complementary components: Unified Reasoning State Modeling (URSM) and Closed-Loop Reasoning-State Learning (CRSL). URSM unifies reasoning construction, correct reasoning preservation, and erroneous reasoning correction as reasoning-state transitions within a shared state space, enabling these heterogeneous reasoning behaviors to be learned jointly. CRSL further incorporates self-generated historical reasoning states during training and recursively updates the current state during inference, thereby mitigating the reasoning-state gap and enabling iterative self-refinement without requiring an additional reflection module, critic, or independent correction stage. Furthermore, we construct VersaCoT-10K, a high-quality CoT dataset spanning eight visual reasoning domains, which provides diverse supervision for multi-domain reasoning and reasoning-state refinement. Extensive experiments demonstrate that ReState exhibits strong adaptability across different MLLM backbones and achieves strong performance on multiple public reasoning benchmarks, supporting the shift from stage-centric reasoning toward state-centric reasoning. Our data and code are available at https://restate-project.github.io

RAMA: Retrieval-Augmented Multi-Agent Routing with Episodic Memory for LEO Satellite Networks
Authors: Xin Ming Li, Peng Hu
First: 2026-10-30T17:26:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:26:50+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Low-Earth orbit (LEO) satellite networks present a challenging routing environment due to rapidly changing topology, traffic, and link conditions. Existing deep reinforcement learning (DRL) routing methods typically rely on current observations and information encoded in network weights, limiting their ability to exploit successful past experiences. We propose RAMA, a retrieval-augmented multi-agent DRL framework with Approximate Information State (AIS), in which each satellite combines its local observation, recent routing history, neighboring state information, and retrieved entries from a per-satellite episodic memory. Retrieved experiences are integrated through a Transformer-based decision network trained with AIS-based auxiliary objectives. With weights frozen in a 140-satellite Kepler constellation, RAMA reduces mean end-to-end latency by 13.4% and 99th-percentile latency by 66.7% relative to the state-of-theart double deep Q-network (DDQN) routing method, mainly by avoiding long detours. Updated online, RAMA remains stable while DDQN degrades. On the 1,584-satellite Starlink shell 1, the reduction in mean latency grows to 93.2%, and updating RAMA online lowers its mean latency by a further 25.5%. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of retrieval-augmented memory for scalable decentralized routing in LEO satellite networks.

Abstract
Autonomous drone fleets have potential in medical supply delivery during disaster incident response. However, coordinating multiple drones in such settings introduces compounding challenges: dynamic hazards such as wind, obstacles, and intermittent connectivity, constrained energy budgets, and the need to serve patients fairly under strict deadlines and triage-based priority. We present CEDA, a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) Double Deep Q-Network algorithm augmented with a QMIX-style monotonic mixing network for cooperative multi-drone medical delivery, designed to jointly optimize triage-aware routing, multi-agent coordination, and energy-efficient navigation under dynamic uncertainty. CEDA introduces a Priority-Preserving Fair Scheduling strategy, in which a structured reward function encodes triage priority weights, outcome-level fairness and priority-ordering rewards, and complementary shaping mechanisms that discourage systematic starvation. We evaluate CEDA in a simulated grid environment with five drones serving up to fifty patients, featuring dynamic hazard zones, stochastic action failures, and dynamically spawning patients across three triage levels, and in PX4 software-in-the-loop (SITL) validation using five X500 quadrotors controlled via MAVSDK. Simulation results show that CEDA delivers over 95\% of spawned patients, reduces obstacle collisions by over 95\% across training, and achieves a weighted triage efficiency exceeding 0.90 alongside a Jain's Fairness Index of approximately 1.0 across triage classes. CEDA preserves clinical priority ordering, with Critical patients receiving the fastest response times, while achieving low and comparable mortality across all triage classes. PX4 SITL validation further shows that the learned policy retains its triage-aware behavior under continuous simulated flight and delayed-action conditions.

DeFT-RAG: Decomposing Multi-Hop Questions into Self-Contained Sub-Questions with Reusable Answers
Authors: Dingyi Kang, Bingzhe Li
First: 2026-10-27T18:40:23+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:12:48+00:00
Comments: 12 pages, 2 figures, 7 tables. Code: https://github.com/Dingyi-Kang/DeFT-RAG
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) lets a large language model (LLM) answer questions by retrieving relevant passages from a large corpus. However, RAG struggles with multi-hop questions, which require chaining facts spread across several documents through intermediate entities that the question never mentions. Graph-based RAG methods retrieve once from the entities a question names and so rarely recover the full evidence chain. Iterative RAG methods recover it step by step, but their accuracy is limited by the many tasks they ask the LLM to perform, such as planning the sub-questions, deciding their dependencies, rewriting them as answers arrive, and keeping track of what is known. We propose DeFT-RAG, a RAG framework built on three components. First, DeFT-RAG generates one self-contained sub-question at a time from the original question and the sub-questions answered so far with their answers (QA pairs). Each sub-question names every entity it asks about, which removes the need to plan, track dependencies or rewrite, and lets the LLM focus on a single fine-grained task at a time. Second, DeFT-RAG moves the remaining LLM work outside the LLM: rule-based updates to a QA table that keeps track of what is known, and yes/no decisions made by a fine-tuned lightweight decision model. Third, DeFT-RAG caches the QA pairs and reuses an answer when a later question asks a sub-question with the same meaning. On MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA and HotpotQA, DeFT-RAG achieves the best accuracy among thirteen baselines with both gpt-4o-mini and Qwen3.6 as the backbone, while giving the LLM fewer input tokens than any other iterative method. With a QA cache shared across 1,000 MuSiQue questions, 23.1% of sub-questions are answered from the cache, which reduces LLM calls by 12.9% without lowering accuracy.

Same Question, Different Answers: Auditing Persona-Dependent Variation in Six LLMs on Smoking-Cessation Advice

Abstract
Hundreds of millions of people now ask large language model (LLM) chatbots for health advice, which raises a question that is simple to pose and hard to answer: does everyone get the same advice? We audit six widely deployed LLMs from four providers on smoking cessation, a domain with codified clini- cal guidance and substantial public-health stakes, by putting the same four cessation questions to twelve user personas that differ in life stage, self-described expertise, and smoking history, for a total of 2,880 responses. What the models say depends on who appears to be asking. Every model named FDA-approved prescription cessation medications less often to teen personas than to adult personas, by 32 to 67 percentage points, and in five of six models a persona presenting health- care expertise received fewer facts than a persona presenting none. We then evaluate one deployable intervention: a short system prompt specifying a factual floor, the set of core med- ical facts every user should receive when they are relevant, while leaving tone, vocabulary, and framing free to adapt. The prompt narrows persona differences in every model, but its effect on overall fact coverage varies in both magnitude and direction across models, so a prompt validated on one system cannot be assumed to transfer to another. Two cautions frame these results. Equal mention rates are not always the correct clinical target, because prescription cessation pharmacother- apy is not recommended for minors, so a defensible factual floor must be age-aware rather than uniform. And because our personas vary on several attributes at once, we report scenario- level differences rather than isolated effects of age or expertise. We release all 2,880 responses and the analysis code.

Abstract
Skill evolution offers a promising way to improve large language model agents without updating their parameters, but its use in formal theorem proving remains underexplored. Existing methods mainly target natural-language reasoning, improving skills by analyzing successful and failed trajectories and incrementally revising solving strategies. Although the Lean verifier provides reliable execution feedback, when all sampled trajectories fail, existing skill evolution methods lack successful trajectories from which to infer effective update directions. Furthermore, these methods also focus mainly on the root instruction file, thus underexploring the evolution of reference knowledge including mathematical concepts and proving techniques. To address these limitations, we propose a mutation-enhanced skill self-evolution framework for building skill-augmented Lean provers. The framework jointly evolves a high-level solving policy and its reference knowledge through progressive and mutation-based updates. Progressive evolution derives local improvements from successful and failed trajectories, while mutation is triggered when no complete proof can be generated, sampling mathematical concepts to produce and select new skill candidates under verifier feedback. We evaluate our method on MiniF2F, PutnamBench, the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO 2025), and the 2026 USA Mathematical Olympiad (USAMO 2026). Under the same backbone model, trajectorysampling budget, and test-time compute, our method achieves proof success rates of 100.0%, 90.6%, 4/6, and 4/6, respectively, with GPT-5.5, outperforming the baseline methods. Further analysis shows that concept-guided mutation outperforms random-text-guided mutation by 6.9 and 8.2 percentage points on MiniF2F and PutnamBench, respectively, while solving one additional problem on both IMO 2025 and USAMO 2026.

Abstract
Five years after the discovery of persistent anti-Muslim bias in large language models, most evaluations remain confined to single-turn prompt completion, a setting that no longer reflects how frontier LLMs are deployed. We introduce \textbf{MIRAGE} (Muslim-Identity Reasoning and Agentic Generation Evaluation), a benchmark of 1{,}200 prompts spanning three deployment-realistic conditions: direct completion, chain-of-thought reasoning, and simulated agentic decision-making across content moderation, lending triage, refugee claim summarization, and hiring screens. Existing prompt-based mitigations transfer poorly across our three conditions, suppressing direct-completion bias while leaving agentic asymmetry largely intact. We release MIRAGE and an open evaluation harness to support targeted mitigation research.

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) can help discover mathematical equations from data, but most LLM-guided symbolic regression methods focus on a single best equation without quantifying uncertainty over alternative functional forms. This is challenging because distinct equations can fit the observed data similarly while producing substantially different extrapolations. Our experiments on the LLM-SRBench dataset show that standard conformal intervals achieve nominal in-distribution coverage but frequently fail under extrapolation. To tackle this challenge, we introduce LLM-SR-UQ, a framework that retains competing equation structures and uses their disagreement to characterize uncertainty about the underlying functional form. We extend the standard fixed-width conformal approach to quantify structural intervals, which measure disagreement among candidate equations and identify where prediction intervals should widen. Extensive experiments show our framework substantially improves coverage attainment by incorporating structural disagreement while preserving in-distribution coverage. Our approach also shows good generalizability when it is applied to other symbolic-regression methods.

Abstract
Multi-turn negotiation remains a challenging problem for large language model (LLM) agents. While LLMs can conduct natural-language dialogue, existing benchmarks predominantly assess only agreement rates, overlooking whether negotiated commitments actually create value once executed. To address this gap, we introduce TradeWar, a multi-country simulation benchmark that translates negotiation dialogues into concrete tariff and trade-share policies, enforces capacity and network constraints during settlement, and evaluates outcomes via an Economic Health Index (EHI). To solve this execution-grounded task, we propose TACTIC (Trade-aware Adaptive Consolidation and Theory-of-mind for International Commerce), an agent that iteratively refines its negotiation strategy across episodes-without updating base LLM weights-by leveraging prior experience, opponent behavior, and past settlement outcomes. These results indicate that CoT deliberation and interaction memory alone do not fully address delayed execution feedback, whereas settlement-grounded adaptation improves long-horizon negotiation in TradeWar.

DialFork: Going Beyond Factuality to Evaluate Procedural Hallucination in Conversational Agents
Authors: Sergio Burdisso, Esaú Villatoro-Tello, Dairazalia Sánchez-Cortés, Yanis Labrak, Jordi Luque, Milos Cernak, Petr Motlicek

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) deployed as conversational agents are increasingly expected to follow procedural logic. While factual hallucination has received extensive attention, a different failure mode has been overlooked: procedural hallucination, in which an agent produces individually fluent and factually plausible utterances while deviating from the intended conversational process. We introduce DialFork, the first benchmark to measure this failure mode on raw dialogue text. DialFork is built by forking conformant dialogues at controlled turn positions and injecting one of four hallucination types, yielding 36,278 dialogues across 24 tasks in 12 domains; 30 human annotators validate the construction. We define three evaluation tasks that characterize procedural deviation at three granularities: how far a set of dialogues deviates from conformant ones (monitoring deployed agents), how far a single dialogue does (procedural retrieval), and at which turn a dialogue first goes off-track (real-time guardrails). For the first two, we propose dialog-flow-aware metrics, FlowKL and FlowScore, that compare dialogues by how they unfold rather than their content. At the dialogue level, FlowScore significantly outperforms all baselines across seven encoders, showing that semantic similarity is no substitute for procedural similarity. We release DialFork under open licenses at https://github.com/idiap/dialfork, hoping the community will measure conversational process alongside content.

Prune Gradually, Adapt Sparsely: Efficient Pruning of Large Language Models at Extreme Sparsity
Authors: Jean Lucien Randrianantenaina, Hung Nguyen, Nasib Ullah, Jinbin Zhang, Nilesh Gupta, Inderjit S. Dhillon, Rohit Babbar
First: 2026-10-30T16:17:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T16:17:43+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Weight pruning can reduce the storage and inference cost of large language models (LLMs), but preserving quality at high sparsity levels remains challenging. Existing methods face a quality-cost trade-off in this regime. One-shot pruning is cheap but degrades sharply as sparsity increases, even when followed by post-pruning recovery. A growing line of work instead optimizes the mask directly from the dense model under the end-to-end objective; among these methods, [Lee et al.,2026], the recent state of the art at extreme sparsity, reaches high quality but at the cost of model-sized optimizer state and auxiliary variables. To address this gap, we propose ETAPS, which prunes gradually and adapts sparsely: it tightens a global sparsity constraint in progressive stages while training a sparse delta with a small, fixed budget. Our key observation is that gradual weight removal keeps each pruning step small enough for sparse adaptation to compensate, allowing the adapted model to provide end-to-end gradients that guide the next pruning decision without model-sized optimizer state. Across six models from 125M to 13B parameters, ETAPS achieves lower perplexity than ELSA at every evaluated sparsity from 50% to 90%, with perplexity reductions of up to 42%. ETAPS is also far cheaper: at 90% sparsity, it prunes LLaMA2-7B on a single H200, where ELSA uses four, with $5.0\times$ smaller total persistent state and 49% fewer GPU-hours. Together, our findings point to a promising direction for achieving high quality in demanding regimes such as extreme sparsity at substantially lower cost.

Abstract
Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have achieved impressive performance across a wide range of reasoning tasks, yet it remains unclear whether their internal representations and decision criteria align with the latent structure of human cognition. In this work, we propose a novel framework to align MLLMs with the multidimensional mental representations underlying human similarity judgements. We build on a previously established behavior-derived semantic embedding space, in which objects are represented along 66 interpretable dimensions derived from 4.70 million human Odd-One-Out (O1O) judgements. For each triplet, we infer the most salient decision dimension and convert it into a dimension-grounded linguistic rationale. This enables MLLMs to learn both the final choice and a justification consistent with the inferred behavioral criterion. Abstract

Abstract
The classic scheduling problem of minimizing the total weight of tardy jobs ($P_m||\sum_jw_jU_j$) asks for a schedule of jobs each with a processing time, weight, and due date on a constant number of parallel identical machines. A job is late in a schedule if it completes after its due date, and an optimal schedule is one where the total weight of all late jobs is minimized. The classic dynamic programming algorithm of Lawler and Moore [Manag.
