OpenVLHarness: Real World Visual Agentic Tool-Use with Multimodal Memory
Authors: Yu Zhou, Cheng-Fu Yang, Rui Sun, Di Wu, Sibo Peng, Mingyang Zhang, Zhen Yang, Ruiyi Zhang, Nanyun Peng, Zhe Gan, Kai-Wei Chang
First: 2026-10-19T17:59:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:59:58+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have made rapid progress on visual reasoning, yet many tasks require evidence that is difficult to obtain through internal reasoning alone: our controlled study on 22 benchmarks shows that simply increasing the inference budget of the base model yields only modest gains. Such tasks demand grounded perception, external knowledge, and numerical measurements obtained through heterogeneous tools, whose outputs must be made interpretable to the model and retained across reasoning steps. We introduce OpenVLHarness, a multimodal reasoning harness organized around a capability layer and persistent multimodal memory. The capability layer combines task-level tool interfaces with evidence rendering, providing consistent access to heterogeneous backends and converting their outputs into model-readable observations. Persistent multimodal memory retains the corresponding images, structured data, and web references as addressable artifacts throughout each query. A coding interface executes generated programs over stored data, enabling computations that combine results from multiple tools. Together, these mechanisms support an iterative process of acquiring, inspecting, and composing multimodal evidence. Evaluated on 22 benchmarks with eight open and proprietary backbones, OpenVLHarness improves average scores by 9.7-12.6 points over matched no-tool baselines, including +11.2 with Qwen3-VL-8B, +9.7 with Kimi K3, and +9.9 with GPT-6 Astra. With GPT-6 Luna at medium reasoning effort, OpenVLHarness outperforms the base model, Codex, and Visual Sketchpad at maximum effort by 7.7-8.5 points while incurring at most 37% of their model-token cost.

CoopUQ: Robust Multi-Robot Social Navigation via Uncertainty-Aware Cooperative Forecasting

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Reinforcement learning enables mobile robots to make fast and scalable navigation decisions in constrained human-shared environments. However, directly extending single-robot approaches to multi-robot settings often fails to ensure safety and efficiency, especially under occlusions and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, where individual robots may overlook potential risks or make inaccurate predictions. Leveraging cooperation effectively for both prediction and navigation remains a major challenge. We propose CoopUQ, a multi-robot social navigation framework that bridges uncertainty-aware cooperative occupancy forecasting and robust decision making. CoopUQ predicts future occupancy grid maps through cross-robot spatiotemporal feature fusion and further leverages forecasts and their uncertainty to guide policy via constrained multi-agent reinforcement learning, where the cost penalizes navigation through regions predicted to be occupied or highly uncertain. This design enables the policy to adapt its risk sensitivity under distribution shifts. Experimental results across multiple Unity3D-based simulation environments with both in-distribution and OOD settings, including diverse layouts, pedestrian densities, and rushing behavior, demonstrate improved prediction performance and safety over representative baselines. Real-robot experiments further demonstrate its robustness across various scenes. Our code and videos are available at https://coopuq.github.io/.

LatComm: Reusable Context Compression for Latent Communication between Large Language Models
Authors: Huawei Lin, Jinting Liu, Tan Yu, Weijie Zhao
First: 2026-10-19T17:58:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:58:41+00:00
Comments: 17 pages, 9 figures, 11 tables. Under Review
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Systems that combine several large language models (LLMs) often use one context for many questions. When the context is passed between models as text, the receiving model processes all of its tokens again for every question. Avoiding this cost requires a context representation that is compact, reusable across questions, and readable by a different LLM. Existing compression and latent communication methods each provide only some of these properties. We introduce LatComm, a Latent Communication interface that provides all three by separating encoding from reading: the context is encoded once without the question, and the receiver selects what to read for each question. A frozen context encoder LLM compresses the context into a fixed-size packet and sends it to a different frozen receiver LLM, which reads the packet through cross-attention, with gates computed from its prompt. On 12 multiple-choice question-answering datasets, LatComm improves average accuracy over single-vector baselines by up to 10.2 points. On QuALITY, it reduces the receiver's input by 57x and answers the questions of an article 3x faster.

LLM-Flock: LLM-Driven Decentralized Multi-Robot Flocking via Influence-Based Consensus with Collision Avoidance
Authors: Peihan Li, Lifeng Zhou
First: 2025-05-10T05:05:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:46:14+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown strong capabilities in reasoning and planning, prompting researchers to explore their use as decentralized decision-makers for multi-robot formation control. However, directly applying LLMs to such tasks often leads to unstable behavior, in which robots may collapse to the centroid of their positions or diverge entirely, due to hallucinated reasoning and limited coordination awareness. We propose LLM-Flock, a decentralized multi-robot flocking framework that addresses these challenges through two key mechanisms: (1) an influence-based consensus protocol, where robots iteratively refine individually generated formation plans by adopting those of more connected neighbors, driving the team toward a unified plan without centralized control, and (2) a two-layer collision avoidance strategy that pairs LLM-based urgency reasoning with a programmatic collision checker and re-prompting mechanism. We evaluate LLM-Flock across five state-of-the-art LLMs, including both closed-source (GPT-5, Claude 4.5) and open-source (Llama3.1-405b, Qwen-Max, DeepSeek-R1) models, demonstrating notable improvements in formation stability and convergence over prior LLM-based methods. Real-world experiments with Crazyflie drones further validate the framework's applicability to physical multi-robot systems.

Abstract
Training a machine learning (ML) model is an optimization process that typically requires a set of examples with ground-truth labels beforehand. Obtaining these ground truths usually requires laborious human effort and can be costly. The scalability of human labeling becomes a concern when there are a large number of data points that need to be labeled. To address this practical issue, we consider an optimization setting where a subset of samples has ground-truth labels, while the remaining data points were labeled by an ML predictor, which could potentially assign incorrect labels to those points. We propose both first-order and zeroth-order stochastic optimization methods for training on datasets consisting of a mix of true-labeled and ML-labeled samples. We further conduct comprehensive empirical studies from training a linear regression model to fine-tuning a large language model (LLM) to showcase the effectiveness of the proposed algorithms for training with ML-labeled data.

Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Market Making on Prediction-Market Order Books
Authors: Skander Lejmi
First: 2026-10-19T17:38:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:38:36+00:00
Comments: 50 pages, 8 figures, 7 tables. Extended version of a paper accepted at ICAIF 2026 Workshop on AI-Driven Market Microstructure. MSc dissertation, London School of Economics and Political Science (2026)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A market maker provides liquidity by quoting prices to buy and sell an asset, earning the spread while managing inventory risk. We study market makers trained with multi-agent reinforcement learning on prediction markets, specifically Polymarket, whose thin order books pose distinct challenges. Three challenges underpin this research: adverse selection may cause a policy trained on real data to stop quoting; the consequences of a quote can arrive many steps after the action; and prediction-market L2 data does not identify individual orders needed for simulation. We address these challenges by reconstructing order-level LOBSTER-format data from Polymarket and training agents in JaxMARL-HFT on 417 real markets. We enrich the baseline market maker's observation space with the book and recent flow, redesign its actions and inventory controls, and propose a cooperative multi-agent decomposition into a quoter and a risk manager trained using recurrent Independent Proximal Policy Optimisation (IPPO) with RNN policies. We investigate reward shaping, particularly reward blending and post-fill markout, to mitigate credit-assignment difficulties between the agents. Our redesigned single-agent market maker quotes more frequently and incurs smaller held-out losses than the retrained JaxMARL-HFT baseline, although the contributions of jointly changed observations, actions and risk constraints are not separately identified. Decomposition alone performs poorly; careful reward design substantially improves the desk relative to the unshaped split and yields our best result. Models are evaluated on realised end-of-episode portfolio value on held-out markets. None establishes positive average portfolio value in periodic held-out evaluation, and each configuration was trained with one seed. The environment, data and protocol lay a foundation for makers that quote many linked markets at once.

Topological Sheaves of Irreducibility: A Theoretical Framework for Multi-Agent Task Obstructions
Authors: Steven Motta, Davide Mambelli, Alessandro Rozza
First: 2026-10-19T17:30:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:30:40+00:00
Comments: 31 pages, 1 figure, 3 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multi-agent systems can differ under identical agents and communication budgets when they support different forms of joint interaction. We introduce Topological Sheaves of Irreducibility (TSI), a sheaf-theoretic framework separating task-level compatibility obstructions from system capability. A task is represented by an interaction complex $K$ and a capacity sheaf $\mathcal{F}$; nonzero $H^n(K;\mathcal{F})$ defines a structural signature, while registered interventions test capability. For cocycle data, cochain realization is solvable exactly when the represented cohomology class vanishes. Examples show that the signature is sheaf-relative, and the degree-1 reduction yields cycle-space witnesses and observation requirements. We evaluate two finite one-round models. Across 15,625 degree-1 inputs, an all-agent additive primitive solves decision and repair exactly; the optimal pairwise system reaches 88.80% on decision, one input above the 88.79% majority-class baseline, and 26.63% on repair. On a degree-2 tetrahedral-boundary task over 531,441 inputs, the same-2-skeleton system retains all four three-agent primitives yet matches the pairwise-only optimum throughout the registered separation window, whereas adding the class-filling four-agent primitive makes both tasks exact. Status-preserving robustness checks retain exact performance but are not cost-matched to the class-restoring ablation. Conditional on the registered primitives and cost models, TSI provides task-relative, falsifiable tests of higher-order interaction.
Summary / 总结
Multi-agent systems can differ under identical agents and communication budgets when they support different forms of joint interaction.

Abstract
Real-world business requests often leave key requirements unstated because frontline practitioners struggle to articulate rules they know from experience. Forward-deployed engineers (FDEs) must elicit these requirements through dialogue and iteratively optimize solutions until they meet customer acceptance criteria. Existing optimization benchmarks typically start from explicit objectives, while clarification benchmarks often introduce information gaps into existing tasks. We introduce FDE-Bench, the first benchmark built from real customer projects to evaluate agents on end-to-end delivery tasks that require interactive requirement clarification and iterative optimization. Its 49 cases span combinatorial optimization and machine learning. Clarification targets are distilled from discussions between frontline practitioners and FDEs. These results show that reliable FDE-level end-to-end delivery remains out of reach for the evaluated agents on underspecified real-world business tasks. Abstract
Public trust in Autonomous Vehicles (AVs) may depend not only on technical success but also on the fairness of their decision making. While a recent trend in AV research involves using general purpose "common sense" models to guide AV decision making, the degree to which these inherit human biases in driving is still understudied. Given that psychology studies have shown human driver biases exist, such as lower pedestrian-yielding rates to Black pedestrians in the US, we argue that analyses of model bias should also be part of AV evaluation. Concretely, in this paper we propose two new bias testing methodologies for Large Language Models (LLMs) and Visual-Language Models (VLMs), "All Else Being Equal" tests and "Self-Consistency" tests, and a new benchmark (FairYield) to assess bias in pedestrian-yielding decisions. Our findings show that the yielding decisions of both LLMs and VLMs can be influenced by models' predictions of pedestrian gender, ethnicity, religion, disability, age, skin tone, and socio-economic status. While the type and degree of bias is different from model to model, we highlight common patterns, and raise questions about the "common sense" model paradigm, particularly the need to either revise the paradigm or address issues of downstream bias.

PACT: Plan-Augmented Coordination via Trace-Graphs for Dynamic Multi-Agent Combinatorial Optimization
Authors: Xiao Zhou, Alex Schutz, Victor-Alexandru Darvariu, Nick Hawes
First: 2026-10-19T16:22:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T16:22:22+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multi-agent approaches have been investigated recently as a means of improving the scalability of reinforcement learning for neural combinatorial optimization. Existing methods assume that all problem instance information is known upfront. However, real-world problems are often dynamic: relevant information is progressively revealed as the agents take actions (e.g., arriving at an edge in a routing problem only to discover it is untraversable). In such cases, existing methods fail to coordinate agents to explore and exploit the progressively revealed information. To address the arising coordination challenge, we introduce PACT (Plan-Augmented Coordination via Trace-graphs). PACT augments the environment graph with auxiliary nodes representing the plans of agents, which are connected to the resource nodes to indicate future dependencies. The proposed message-passing scheme over the augmented graph grounds inter-agent coordination in the environment and enables proactive, rather than reactive, conflict resolution. We show that PACT improves solution quality in dynamic problems relative to baselines and exhibits the best transfer to larger instances on which, remarkably, it outperforms centralized oracles with privileged access to instance uncertainty.

Abstract
Real-world hybrid web tasks need both information gathering and page interaction, yet search-based and operation-based agents are developed and evaluated separately. To bridge this gap, we introduce HyWe, a framework that unifies both capabilities within one model and trajectory. Its two modes use dedicated prompts and tool sets to sustain sub-goal execution; explicit switching selects the needed capability, and shared history lets each mode build on the other's findings. To provide demonstrations of these behaviors, we develop HyWeCollector, a fully automatic pipeline that uses task prototypes, four switching patterns, and multi-agent synthesis to produce verified hybrid trajectories. We train HyWe-35B on these demonstrations to unify efficient information gathering and browser interaction in a single 35B web agent. To evaluate hybrid task completion, we introduce HyWeBench: 100 human-written tasks on websites distinct from training, with human-written sub-goal checklists and an outcome-based evaluation pipeline. Hybrid training enables HyWe-35B to achieve 47.0% success on this benchmark, improving on the untrained base model by 19.0 percentage points and outperforming existing search-based and operation-based agents. Even before training, the same base model solves 28.0% of tasks with HyWe, versus 11.0% with all tools under one prompt. Beyond hybrid tasks, HyWe-35B achieves 59.0% and 88.3% success on Online-Mind2Web and WebVoyager, outperforming existing open-source baselines and cutting action cost per successful task by 48.3% and 51.9% compared with Fara-7B.

Reasoning-Aware Multicalibrated Reliability Predictors for Language Models
Authors: Ekaterina Fedorova, Alessandro Morosini, Chara Podimata
First: 2026-10-19T16:06:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T16:06:33+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
As people increasingly rely on large language models (LLMs) for information, model providers need to communicate meaningful estimates of whether individual responses are correct or, more generally, reliable. In the context of hallucinations, existing work uses multicalibration to make such reliability predictions meaningful across application-relevant groups, such as prompt topics. Topic groups alone, however, can conceal systematic errors: within the same topic and confidence level, responses with different chain-of-thought (CoT) properties may exhibit opposing errors that cancel on average. In this work, we introduce reasoning-aware multicalibration, a framework that supplements application-relevant topic groups with groups derived from observable properties of CoT traces. We highlight that, theoretically, groupings based on CoT are "internally important", i.e., groupings for which the multicalibration guarantee is not societally crucial as it is for high-risk topics, but whose inclusion in the multicalibration procedure could easily and significantly improve predictive accuracy of the final reliability predictor. Empirically, we apply our framework to hallucination prediction and unsafe-response detection across a variety of benchmarks, and find that adding CoT groups to topic-based calibration reduces Brier score in both tasks. Interestingly, we find that multicalibrating with respect to CoT groups alone (without any topic information) already improves calibration across topics, suggesting that the predictor's errors depend on how the model reasoned, rather than what it was asked about in the prompt.

LSTMem: Hierarchical Long Short-Term Online Memory for Large Language Models
Authors: Xianglong Shi, Ruijie Yang, Sirui Zhao, Shukang Yin, Zihao Bian, Tinghao Yi, Enhong Chen
First: 2026-09-27T06:24:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T15:35:15+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Large language models increasingly serve as long-horizon assistants and agents, where they must both accumulate information across interactions and make the relevant parts available when later requests depend on them. Existing compact online memories typically use a single persistent state both to accumulate history and to serve readout, so what the memory stores cannot be controlled separately from what it exposes to the current computation. We propose LSTMem, an LSTM-inspired online memory that instead equips each layer of a frozen LLM with two matrix-valued states: a cell state that accumulates history and a hidden state whose readouts correct the backbone's attention. Input and forget gates control what the cell stores, while an output gate separately controls what the cell exposes through the hidden state. LSTMem further connects memory across depth through forward hidden-state propagation and block-end feedback, and uses higher-layer reconstruction gradients to refine lower-layer cell states before rebuilding hidden states from shallow to deep layers. Across memory benchmarks on Qwen3-4B-Instruct, LSTMem consistently improves MemoryAgentBench, LoCoMo, and HotpotQA over the plain backbone. Comparisons further show that the LSTM-based memory formulation outperforms an associative-memory counterpart, while removing cross-layer hidden-memory propagation degrades performance. These results demonstrate the benefits of separating memory accumulation from memory expression and organizing memory hierarchically across model depth. The code is available at https://github.com/Longchentong/LSTMem.

Authors: Guowei Xu, Zhenting Qi, Amani Kiruga, Himabindu Lakkaraju, Yilun Du

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The capabilities of an agent swarm depend on both its individual members and how they coordinate. Yet existing multi-agent systems often train agents within fixed orchestration structures, separating the learning of individual capabilities from the organization of collective behavior. We introduce market-based post-training (MPT), a framework for jointly learning action policies and self-orchestration in decentralized agent swarms. Agents express their willingness to participate through bids, which determine who acts and how collective outcomes provide local training credit. Learning both action and bidding behavior allows individual capabilities and patterns of participation to adapt together without a central coordinating agent. We evaluate MPT on embodied crafting, robot navigation, and deep research. Results show gains over monolithic post-training and confirm that agent participation indeed evolves with learning. With the same post-trained action policies, learned self-orchestration outperforms alternative coordination strategies. Ablations show that both action and bidding benefit from learning, and that learned bids provide more effective action-training signals. Beyond the original training objectives, trained swarms adapt faster to related tasks and achieve better performance with larger populations at inference time. Larger populations also suffer smaller performance losses under agent dropout, demonstrating that learned self-orchestration supports reuse, scaling, and robustness in decentralized agent swarms. More details are available on our project page at https://guoweixu.com/mpt/.

Abstract
Participating in a data space still requires the Dataspace Protocol (DSP) negotiation flow to be hand-coded against each provider's access policy. We present a multi-agent architecture that automates this process using natural language requests. Built on LangGraph over an Eclipse Dataspace Components (EDC), the design separates concerns into cooperating agents. The orchestrator's role is to route and plan. Beyond Behavioral Accuracy: Evaluating Individual-Level Fidelity in LLM Investor Simulation
Authors: Jiajie He, Yuanhong Jiang, Xintong Chen, Dongling Ni, Wenjin Liu

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used as user simulators, but can they faithfully reproduce an individual's behavior and decision logic over time? We investigate this question in a longitudinal paper-trading study with 80 participants. Using rolling next-day predictions, we evaluate trading activity, buy-sell structure, asset selection, and portfolio outcomes, complemented by controlled input ablations and participant-rated explanation alignment. No evaluated LLM reliably outperforms a simple recent-activity persistence baseline for predicting trade occurrence, while buy-sell structure and asset selection remain difficult to reproduce. Similar activity-level performance can also yield substantially different portfolio trajectories. Ablations show that activity predictions depend primarily on recent trades, whereas asset choices are sensitive to multiple evidence sources. Participants rate explanations as more aligned with their recurring strategies than with their decision-specific rationales. These findings reveal a gap between coarse behavioral agreement and individual-level fidelity, motivating separate evaluation of behavioral accuracy, explanation alignment, and evidence dependence in personalized user simulation.

RouterWorld: Scaling Conversational AI with Latency-Aware LLM Routing
Authors: Jinyang Li, Denis Emelin, Simone Tedeschi, Weiwei Cheng, Cristina Morariu, Haytham Assem, Jens Lehmann, Emine Yilmaz, Angus Addlesee
First: 2026-10-19T14:45:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T14:45:25+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Conversational AI systems built on Large Language Models (LLMs) require routing mechanisms that balance answer quality and response latency at scale. However, existing LLM routing benchmarks assume full observability of all candidate model outcomes, under-emphasize latency as a first-class metric, and fragment evaluation across heterogeneous protocols misaligned with real-world settings. We introduce RouterWorld, a comprehensive benchmark for LLM routing in conversational GenAI. RouterWorld unifies 371k single-turn responses and 76k multi-turn agentic trajectories, adopts natural user interactions with a standardized LLM-as-a-judge protocol, and decouples efficiency from transient pricing via hardware-grounded latency annotations. Crucially, RouterWorld formalizes routing under partial observability through two setups: Cold-Start, which restricts learning to sparse, static logs, and Voyager, which models continuous data streams where routing decisions shape future training distributions. Abstract
